Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This document specifies the complete software requirements for HostelBite, a Hostel Food Delivery Application designed to digitize food ordering, mess subscription management, and last-mile delivery within university hostel campuses. Version 1.0 of this Software Requirements Specification (SRS) covers the entirety of the proposed system — not a partial subsystem — including the student-facing mobile and web application, the vendor and mess-committee management dashboard, the delivery-personnel interface, and the platform's administrative backend.
The purpose of this document is threefold. First, it serves as a contract of understanding between the development team (the authors) and the project stakeholders — the hostel mess committee, empaneled food vendors, and university administration acting as the informal client for this academic project — regarding exactly what the system will and will not do. Second, it acts as the primary technical reference from which system design, database schema, API contracts, and test plans will be derived in subsequent phases of the Software Engineering Lab coursework. Third, it functions as a baseline for verification and validation, allowing each requirement to be traced to a corresponding test case and, eventually, to an implemented and demonstrably working feature during project evaluation.
This SRS assumes no prior familiarity with the system on the part of the reader and is written to be self-contained, with all domain-specific terminology defined in Appendix A.


1.2 Document Conventions

Requirement statements in this document use the keyword "shall" to denote requirements that are mandatory for the system to be considered complete, and "should" to denote requirements that are desirable but not strictly mandatory for the Version 1.0 release. The word "may" is used sparingly to denote genuinely optional behavior left to implementer discretion.
Each functional requirement is uniquely and permanently identified using the format FR-<Module>-<Number> (e.g., FR-ORD-01 for the first functional requirement under the Order Placement module), so that it can be referenced unambiguously from design documents, test cases, and the traceability matrix in Appendix D. Nonfunctional requirements follow the parallel convention NFR-<Category>-<Number> (e.g., NFR-PERF-03), and business rules follow BR-<Number>.
Priority levels — High, Medium, or Low — are assigned at the feature level in Section 4 and are inherited by all individual requirements under that feature unless explicitly overridden at the requirement level with a bracketed priority tag. Bold text is used to denote terms that are formally defined in Appendix A (Glossary) the first time they appear in a given section. Tables are numbered sequentially per section (e.g., Table 2.3-1) and listed in the List of Tables on Page 2.

1.4 Product Scope

HostelBite aims to eliminate the inefficiencies of manual, phone-call-based, register-entry, or walk-in food ordering within hostel campuses by providing a single unified digital platform connecting three primary actors: students/residents, mess and local food vendors, and delivery personnel, with a fourth supervisory actor, the Hostel Administrator, overseeing platform-wide operations.
Presently, most hostel messes at Manipal University Jaipur and comparable institutions rely on a combination of physical meal-count registers, WhatsApp group broadcasts for daily menus, and informal phone-call ordering for outside vendors. This creates several documented pain points, gathered directly from stakeholder interviews conducted by the authors in August 2026: (a) mess committees routinely over-prepare or under-prepare food because they lack advance visibility into how many residents intend to eat a given meal; (b) students frequently wait in physical queues or make repeated phone calls to vendors during peak hours with no order-status visibility; (c) payment is almost entirely cash-based, creating reconciliation difficulty for vendors and security/logistics concerns for students; and (d) there is no structured mechanism for students to provide feedback on food quality that vendors or the mess committee can act upon systematically.
The system will allow students to browse the daily mess menu and empaneled third-party vendor menus side by side, place orders with item customization and delivery scheduling, pay digitally through UPI, card, or an in-app prepaid hostel wallet, track their delivery from acceptance through hand-off in real time, and rate their experience post-delivery. Vendors and the mess committee will manage their menus, toggle item availability, and process the incoming order queue through a dedicated web dashboard. The Hostel Administrator will approve new vendors, resolve escalated disputes, and access campus-wide analytics for mess planning and vendor performance evaluation.
The product's core measurable objectives, agreed with stakeholders, are: (1) reducing average order-to-delivery wait time relative to the current phone-call-based process; (2) minimizing food wastage by giving the mess committee advance visibility into expected meal counts; (3) providing a cashless transaction experience suited to a student population that increasingly expects digital payment; and (4) giving hostel administration structured analytics that were previously unavailable through manual processes.
Explicitly out of scope for Version 1.0: inter-campus or off-campus delivery beyond the hostel boundary; aggregation of third-party restaurants not formally empaneled through the vendor onboarding workflow (Section 4.2); AI-based meal recommendation or personalization; and any hardware-based kiosk or POS terminal deployment. These exclusions are revisited as candidate future-scope items in Appendix C.

Overall Description
Product Perspective
HostelBite is conceived as a new, self-contained product; it is not a modification or extension of any existing commercial or institutional software system, though it functionally replaces the informal manual processes currently used by hostel mess committees, namely register-based meal counting and WhatsApp-broadcast-based vendor ordering described in Section 1.4.
The system follows a three-tier client-server architecture, chosen specifically for its clear separation of concerns and its suitability to the two-person development team's timeline and skillset:
Presentation Tier: A cross-platform mobile application built using Flutter, targeting both students and delivery personnel, and a responsive web-based dashboard built using React, targeting vendors and administrators. Flutter was chosen over native Android/iOS development specifically to allow a two-developer team to maintain a single codebase across both major mobile platforms within a single academic semester.
Application Tier: A RESTful API backend built on Node.js with the Express framework, responsible for all business logic, authentication and authorization, order-state orchestration, and coordination with external payment and notification services.
Data Tier: A PostgreSQL relational database serving as the system of record for transactional data — users, orders, menus, and payments — supplemented by a Redis in-memory store used for session management, caching of frequently-read data such as the daily menu, and real-time publish/subscribe messaging that powers live order-status updates.

The system interfaces with several external services rather than implementing this functionality natively, in keeping with sound software engineering practice of not reinventing well-solved problems: a payment gateway (Razorpay, supporting UPI, cards, and net banking) for all monetary transactions; a push-notification service (Firebase Cloud Messaging) for real-time alerts to students, vendors, and delivery agents; an SMS gateway as a fallback notification channel for critical transactional messages when push delivery cannot be confirmed; and, where institutional cooperation permits, the university's existing student directory for cross-verification of roll numbers during registration.
Figure 2.1-1: System Context Diagram (described). At the center of the system sits the API Gateway, which routes requests to four logical backend services: the User Service (authentication, profile, and role management), the Order Service (cart, checkout, and order-state machine), the Payment Service (gateway integration, wallet ledger, and refund processing), and the Notification Service (push and SMS dispatch). The Student Mobile App and Delivery Agent App both connect to the API Gateway over HTTPS and maintain a persistent WebSocket connection for real-time order-status updates. The Vendor/Admin Web Dashboard connects similarly but does not require the WebSocket channel for most operations, relying instead on periodic polling supplemented by push notifications for new-order alerts. All four backend services read from and write to the shared PostgreSQL database, while the Order Service additionally uses Redis for its real-time pub/sub channel.
This architectural separation means that, for example, an outage or slowdown in the Payment Service's connection to the external gateway does not prevent students from browsing menus or vendors from managing their catalog — a deliberate resilience decision informed by the team's coursework in distributed systems design. A full architecture diagram with component boundaries is maintained separately in the project's technical design repository and is referenced here in keeping with IEEE 830 Appendix B conventions for supporting analysis models.


Product Functions
At a summary level — with full elaboration deferred to the individual functional requirements in Section 4 — HostelBite shall provide the following major categories of functionality, organized around the natural workflow of a hostel food order from account creation through post-delivery feedback:
Account management: Registration, OTP-based verification, login, and profile management for all four primary actor types (students, vendors, delivery agents, administrators), with hostel-block and room-number capture specific to the student role for delivery-address purposes.
Menu discovery: Daily mess-menu display refreshed each morning, empaneled third-party vendor catalog browsing, text search, and filtering by cuisine type, price range, and dietary classification (vegetarian, non-vegetarian, vegan, Jain).
Order placement: Shopping-cart management with quantity adjustment, item customization notes, delivery-slot scheduling (immediate or advance), delivery-point selection within the hostel, and a streamlined order-confirmation flow.
Payment processing: Support for UPI, debit/credit card payment via the integrated gateway, and a dedicated in-app prepaid wallet supporting manual top-up as well as automatic recurring debit for mess-subscription billing.
Delivery coordination: Automatic or manual assignment of ready orders to available delivery personnel, live order-status broadcasting to all relevant actors, and delivery-point-aware routing within the hostel premises.
Notifications: Multi-channel (push-first, SMS-fallback) alerts at every meaningful order-state transition, plus optional promotional notifications that students may opt out of independently from transactional alerts.
Feedback loop: Post-delivery star ratings and written reviews, aggregated into a vendor-level score visible to future customers, with a single vendor-response allowance per review.
Vendor and mess operations: Full menu CRUD (create, read, update, delete) functionality, real-time order-queue management presented as a Kanban-style board, and instantaneous item-availability toggling to reflect kitchen realities such as running out of a dish.
Administrative oversight: Vendor-application review and approval workflow, complaint escalation and dispute resolution tooling, manual refund authorization above a configured threshold, and campus-wide analytics dashboards covering order volume, revenue, and estimated food wastage.
Complaint handling: A structured, SLA-bound complaint-raising and resolution pathway distinct from the general ratings-and-reviews feedback loop, intended specifically for order-level issues requiring remediation such as refunds.
These nine function categories map directly to the eleven system features elaborated with individual, testable functional requirements beginning in Section 4 (menu discovery and order placement additionally span cart management as a connective sub-function).

Because the largest and most frequent user class (students) is assumed to have limited patience for a complex or slow interface, the mobile application is deliberately designed to prioritize a maximum three-tap ordering flow from menu browsing to order confirmation, as formalized in Section 3.1 (UI-02). Operating Environment
HostelBite's student-facing and delivery-agent applications shall run on Android 9.0 (API level 28) and above, and iOS 14.0 and above, chosen as the minimum supported versions based on an informal survey of device generations currently in use among hostel residents, which indicated negligible usage of older OS versions within the target population. The vendor and administrator dashboard shall run within modern desktop and tablet web browsers — specifically the latest two major stable releases of Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, and Microsoft Edge — at a minimum viewport resolution of 1024×768, with graceful responsive degradation down to tablet-sized viewports of 768×1024 to accommodate vendors who may prefer managing orders from a tablet placed at their food counter.

The backend services shall be deployed on a Linux-based cloud environment running Ubuntu 22.04 LTS, using containerized services orchestrated via Docker (and Docker Compose for the development environment, with potential migration to a lightweight Kubernetes setup should the project extend beyond a single-campus pilot). The specific cloud provider shall be selected based on cost considerations appropriate to an academic project budget, with a preference for providers offering a free or low-cost student tier (e.g., a low-cost VPS provider or a platform-as-a-service option), while ensuring the chosen provider supports the campus's data-residency expectations for student personal information.
Design and Implementation Constraints
The following constraints were identified during requirements gathering and shall bound all subsequent design and implementation decisions for Version 1.0 of the system:
Network reliability constraint: The system must operate acceptably under intermittent hostel Wi-Fi conditions, which stakeholder interviews confirmed to be a recurring issue in several hostel blocks. Core screens — specifically the menu-browsing and cart screens — must support local caching of previously-loaded data with automatic synchronization once connectivity is restored, so that a student can continue browsing a cached menu even during a brief network drop.
Regulatory payment constraint: All payment processing, and specifically the in-app prepaid wallet, must comply with Reserve Bank of India guidelines governing Prepaid Payment Instruments (PPIs), including the maximum permissible stored balance and any applicable KYC requirements for wallet usage beyond minimal-KYC thresholds.
PCI-DSS scope minimization constraint: The platform must not store raw card numbers, CVV codes, or any other data that would bring the system into full PCI-DSS scope; all card transactions must be tokenized and processed entirely through the integrated payment gateway's hosted checkout or SDK.
Technology stack constraint: The mobile application must be built using Flutter, and the backend must be built using Node.js with Express, both decisions driven by the existing skillset of the two-person development team and by prior coursework alignment within the B.Tech Information Technology curriculum, ensuring the team can realistically deliver a working system within one academic semester.
Deployment scale constraint: The system is deliberately constrained to a single-campus, single-tenant deployment model for Version 1.0; a multi-campus or multi-tenant architecture supporting multiple universities simultaneously is explicitly deferred to a future version and tracked as TBD-item in Appendix C, to avoid over-engineering within the available project timeline.
Timeline constraint: Development is constrained to the duration of a single academic semester as governed by the Software Engineering Lab course schedule at Manipal University Jaipur, which directly influenced the decision to scope Version 1.0 to a single-hostel pilot rather than a full multi-hostel campus rollout.
Team-size constraint: With only two developers (the authors) responsible for the full stack — mobile, backend, and dashboard — feature prioritization in Section 4 favors depth in the core ordering and delivery flow over breadth of secondary features such as advanced analytics or promotional-code systems.
User Documentation
The following user-facing documentation artifacts shall be delivered alongside the software, each targeted at a specific user class identified in Section 2.3:
In-app onboarding tutorial — a brief, skippable first-launch walkthrough for the Student role, covering account registration, menu browsing, and the first order-placement flow, presented as a maximum of five swipeable screens.
Vendor Dashboard User Guide (PDF) — a document covering menu management, order-queue handling, item-availability toggling, and payout/settlement understanding, written in plain, non-technical language suitable for small-business vendor owners.
Administrator Manual (PDF) — covering the vendor-approval workflow, complaint-escalation handling, refund-authorization process, and how to interpret the analytics dashboard for mess-planning decisions.
Delivery Agent Quick-Start Card — a single-page printable/digital reference covering how to accept assigned orders, update delivery status, and use the in-app navigation aid to locate hostel rooms.
In-app FAQ / Help Section — accessible from the Profile tab of the student application, covering common issues such as payment failures, order cancellation policy, and how to raise a complaint.
All documentation shall be authored in Markdown for ease of version control alongside the codebase, and shall be exported to PDF for distribution to non-technical stakeholders such as vendors and the mess committee. Documentation shall be updated in lockstep with any functional change to the corresponding feature, and each documentation artifact's version number shall be kept synchronized with the software release it accompanies.

Assumptions and Dependencies
The following assumptions underlie the requirements specified in this document, and their invalidation would necessitate a revision of scope or approach:
It is assumed that the hostel campus provides sufficiently reliable indoor and outdoor Wi-Fi coverage to support order placement and real-time status updates during normal operation, notwithstanding the intermittent-connectivity resilience built in per Section 2.5.
It is assumed that the target student population possesses either a smartphone with UPI-enabled banking access, or is willing to adopt the platform's prepaid wallet as an alternative, given that a meaningful subset of first-year students may not yet have activated UPI on their personal bank accounts.
It is assumed that at least one delivery-personnel resource is realistically available per hostel block during core operational hours (7:00 AM to 11:00 PM), whether sourced from existing mess staff, part-time student employment, or a dedicated hired delivery team — the specific staffing model being outside the scope of this software specification.
It is assumed that empaneled vendors are willing and able to maintain reasonably prompt digital menu updates; a vendor who never updates item availability will degrade the accuracy of the platform's real-time stock information but will not cause a system failure.
The system's core functionality carries hard dependencies on the continued availability and API stability of the following third-party services: the Razorpay (or equivalent) payment gateway for all monetary transactions; Firebase Cloud Messaging for push notification delivery; and an SMS gateway provider for fallback notification delivery. Any sustained outage in these external dependencies will degrade, but per the resilience design in Section 2.1, not entirely halt, core platform functionality such as menu browsing.

External Interface Requirements
User Interfaces
The student mobile application shall present four primary screens accessible via a persistent bottom navigation bar, chosen as the dominant mobile navigation pattern for frequently-used, flat (non-hierarchical) feature sets: Home/Menu, Cart, Orders (live tracking and history), and Profile/Wallet. The overall visual design shall follow Material Design 3 guidelines when rendered on Android and shall approximate Apple's Human Interface Guidelines conventions when rendered on iOS, leveraging Flutter's adaptive widget capabilities to achieve platform-appropriate look and feel without maintaining two separate codebases.
The following specific interface requirements shall govern the student, vendor, and delivery-agent interfaces:
UI-01: The Home screen shall display the current day's hostel mess menu (for subscribed students) positioned above the listing of empaneled vendors, with vendors sorted by a combination of estimated delivery time and the student's hostel-block proximity.
UI-02: The complete cart-to-checkout flow shall not exceed three taps from initial item selection to final order confirmation, measured from the item detail screen's "Add to Cart" action through to the confirmation receipt screen.
UI-03: The Order Tracking screen shall present order progress using a horizontal stepper visualization with five discrete states: Placed → Accepted → Preparing → Out for Delivery → Delivered, with the currently active state visually emphasized and prior states shown with completion timestamps.
UI-04: All monetary values displayed anywhere in the application shall be formatted in Indian Rupees (₹) with two decimal places of precision, following standard Indian currency formatting conventions (e.g., ₹1,250.00).
UI-05: The vendor dashboard shall present its incoming order queue as a Kanban-style board with four columns — New, In Progress, Ready, and Completed — allowing a vendor to visually triage order volume at a glance during peak periods without needing to open individual order records.
UI-06: All interactive elements and text within the application shall meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA color-contrast requirements, and the application shall support a user-adjustable font-scaling setting within Profile settings to accommodate students with visual impairment. 
UI-07: The Cart screen shall display a running subtotal, applicable delivery fee, and platform fee as individually itemized line items before the final total, ensuring pricing transparency and avoiding surprise charges at checkout. 
UI-08: The Delivery Agent application shall present assigned tasks in a simplified, single-column list view sorted by pickup urgency, deliberately avoiding the more complex multi-panel dashboard layout used by vendors, in recognition of the delivery agent's typically brief, on-the-go interaction pattern with the app. 
UI-09: Error states (e.g., payment failure, network timeout) shall be communicated using plain, non-technical language with a clear, single primary recovery action (such as "Retry Payment" or "Check Connection"), avoiding raw error codes or stack traces in any user-facing surface. 
UI-10: The Administrator dashboard shall present a summary landing page ("Overview") showing today's order volume, pending vendor approvals, and open complaints as at-a-glance cards before requiring navigation into more detailed sub-views.

Wireframe-level mockups corresponding to each of the above requirements are maintained separately in the project's design repository (Figma source files) and are referenced but not reproduced within this textual specification, in keeping with the convention that an SRS specifies what the interface must achieve rather than dictating exact pixel-level layout, which is a design-phase concern.
Hardware Interfaces
HostelBite has no requirement to interface with specialized or dedicated hardware devices in Version 1.0; the system is designed to run entirely on commodity consumer smartphones and standard web-browsing hardware, in keeping with the project's constrained development timeline and the goal of minimizing deployment friction for the pilot hostel.
The following hardware interface requirements apply specifically to smartphone sensors accessed through the mobile operating system's standard APIs, rather than any purpose-built or external hardware:
HW-01: The Delivery Agent application shall interface with the host smartphone's GPS module to obtain and periodically transmit the agent's live location while an order is in the "Out for Delivery" state, with a required positional accuracy of within 15 meters to support meaningful in-hostel location display.
HW-02: The Delivery Agent application shall interface with the host smartphone's camera module to support an optional proof-of-delivery photograph, captured at the point of hand-off, which shall be attached to the order record for dispute-resolution purposes.
HW-03: The Student and Delivery Agent applications shall interface with the host smartphone's camera module to support QR-code scanning as a lightweight order-pickup and delivery-verification mechanism, serving as an alternative to manual status confirmation and reducing the possibility of a delivery agent marking an order delivered before actual hand-off.
HW-04: The Student application shall interface with the host smartphone's push-notification subsystem (as exposed through the operating system, via Firebase Cloud Messaging) to receive real-time order-status alerts even when the application is not in the foreground.
No point-of-sale (POS) terminal, dedicated barcode scanner, or printer-based receipt hardware is required for Version 1.0; digital receipts are considered sufficient given the target student demographic's comfort with fully digital transaction records, and the QR-code-based verification mechanism described in HW-03 was deliberately chosen as a low-cost software-only alternative to a hardware barcode scanner.

Communications Interfaces
All communication between the mobile and web clients and the backend API shall occur exclusively over HTTPS using TLS version 1.2 or higher; plaintext HTTP shall not be permitted for any endpoint under any circumstance, including during local development, in order to enforce security discipline from the earliest stages of implementation.
Real-time order-status updates — a core differentiator of the platform relative to the existing phone-call-based process — shall be delivered using a WebSocket connection established via the Socket.IO library, chosen specifically for its built-in automatic fallback to HTTP long-polling on restrictive hostel network configurations that may block raw WebSocket upgrade requests (a scenario the authors confirmed occurs on at least one campus network segment during preliminary testing).
All request and response payloads exchanged between client and server shall be formatted as JSON, adhering to a documented OpenAPI 3.0 specification maintained as a separate artifact from this SRS within the project's technical repository, ensuring that API contract details (exact field names, types, and validation rules) remain independently versionable from this higher-level requirements document.
Additional communications interface requirements:
COMM-01: The system shall use a single, versioned API base path (e.g., /api/v1/) to allow for backward-compatible evolution of the API contract as the project progresses beyond Version 1.0.
COMM-02: All API error responses shall follow a consistent JSON error envelope containing an error code, a human-readable message, and (where applicable) a field-level validation detail array, to simplify consistent client-side error handling across the mobile app and web dashboard.
COMM-03: The WebSocket channel shall implement automatic reconnection with exponential backoff on connection loss, ensuring that a brief network interruption does not require the user to manually refresh the application to resume receiving live order updates.
COMM-04: Webhook callbacks received from the payment gateway shall be cryptographically verified using the gateway's provided signature-verification mechanism before being trusted, to prevent forged payment-confirmation requests.

5.2 Safety Requirements
NFR-SAFE-01: The system shall clearly display allergen information, where provided by the vendor, on every menu item's detail page.
NFR-SAFE-02: The system shall prevent new orders for any item marked unavailable due to a declared food-safety recall, and shall alert any student with a pending order for a recalled item.
NFR-SAFE-03: The system shall not process any payment without an explicit, distinct confirmation action, preventing accidental financial loss.
NFR-SAFE-04: In the event of a failure during payment processing, the system shall ensure idempotent transaction handling such that no partial or duplicate charge is applied.
NFR-SAFE-05: The system shall require a delivery agent to confirm the correct delivery point before marking an order “Delivered.”
5.3 Security Requirements
NFR-SEC-01: All data transmitted between client and server shall be encrypted using TLS 1.2 or higher, with no exceptions.
NFR-SEC-02: All passwords shall be stored using bcrypt hashing with a minimum cost factor of 12.
NFR-SEC-03: The system shall implement role-based access control (RBAC) strictly limiting each user class to its authorized functionality.
NFR-SEC-04: The system shall log all administrative actions to an immutable audit log retained for a minimum of 12 months.
NFR-SEC-05: The system shall comply with applicable provisions of India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.
NFR-SEC-06: The system shall implement rate limiting on all authentication-related endpoints.
NFR-SEC-07: The system shall sanitize and validate all user-submitted input to prevent SQL injection and stored XSS.
NFR-SEC-08: The system shall require re-authentication before sensitive account actions such as a change of registered mobile number.

NFR-QUAL-02 (Reliability): The order-processing service shall achieve MTBF exceeding 720 hours during pilot operation.

6. Other Requirements
Legal and Regulatory Requirements
The platform shall retain complete transaction records for a minimum of 8 years, in compliance with applicable Indian tax and financial record-keeping regulations governing digital payment platforms. Vendor FSSAI license validity, as established in Business Rule BR-10, shall remain a mandatory and continuously-enforced precondition for a vendor's continued active status on the platform.
Internationalization Requirements
Not applicable for Version 1.0; the system targets a single-language (English) deployment consistent with the language of instruction at the target university. A future version may introduce Hindi-language support, tracked as an open item in Appendix C.
Reuse Objectives
The Payment Service and Notification Service are deliberately designed as loosely-coupled, independently-deployable modules to enable their reuse across potential future Manipal University Jaipur digital initiatives — for example, a general-purpose campus marketplace — subject to separate architectural review beyond the scope of this SRS.
Installation Requirements
The student and delivery-agent applications shall be made distributable via the Google Play Store and Apple App Store for the eventual production release. A sideloadable Android APK shall additionally be provided for initial pilot testing within a single hostel block prior to public store submission, allowing faster iteration during the academic-project evaluation period.
Licensing Requirements
All third-party libraries and frameworks incorporated into the system — including Flutter, React, and all Node.js package dependencies — shall be verified for MIT, Apache 2.0, or otherwise compatible open-source licensing prior to inclusion, and a complete license manifest shall be maintained within the project's version-control repository for audit purposes.
Cultural and Political Requirements
The system shall accommodate dietary restrictions relevant to the target population, including but not limited to Jain dietary requirements (no root vegetables), consistent with the dietary-tag filtering established in FR-MEN-02.

Appendix B: Analysis Models
B.1 Use Case Diagram (described)
The system's primary actors are Student, Vendor, Delivery Agent, Mess Committee, and Administrator. Key use cases include: Register/Login, Browse Menu, Place Order, Make Payment, Track Order, Rate Order, Raise Complaint, Manage Menu (Vendor), Fulfill Order (Vendor), Accept/Decline Delivery (Delivery Agent), Approve Vendor (Administrator), Resolve Complaint (Administrator), and View Analytics (Administrator). The Student actor has an “includes” relationship from Place Order to Make Payment, and an “extends” relationship from Track Order to Raise Complaint, reflecting that a complaint is an optional, conditional continuation of the tracking flow rather than a mandatory step.
B.2 Entity-Relationship Overview (described)
Core entities are User (related 1:1 to Student/Vendor/DeliveryAgent/Admin subtypes via a role attribute), MenuItem (many-to-one with Vendor), Order (many-to-one with both Student and Vendor; one-to-many with OrderItem), Payment (one-to-one with Order), WalletTransaction (many-to-one with User), Complaint (one-to-one with Order), and Review (one-to-one with Order). Referential integrity is enforced such that an Order record cannot exist without valid foreign keys to both a Student and a Vendor entity, and a Payment record is created only upon a successful, cryptographically-verified gateway webhook callback (per COMM-04).
B.3 Order Lifecycle State Diagram (described)
The Order entity's status field transitions through a strict state machine: Payment Pending → Placed → Accepted → Preparing → Ready → Out for Delivery → Delivered, with permitted side-branches to Cancelled (only from Placed) and to Disputed (only from Delivered, within the 12-hour complaint window per BR-09). No state transition may skip an intermediate state, ensuring the order-tracking UI (UI-03) always reflects an accurate, sequential progression.
Diagram artifacts — the full use case diagram, entity-relationship diagram, and order-lifecycle state diagram in graphical UML/Mermaid form — are maintained as separate source files within the project repository and are referenced here per IEEE 830 Appendix B convention.
